---
title: "Common Spec Structure"
description: "The minimum contract kernel required by every Hiaka specification."
---

<Note>**Status: draft.** This structure is normative but not yet stable.</Note>

Every Hiaka specification MUST use this common structure and declare one
primary [specification profile](/framework).

A section MAY be marked `Not applicable` only when the specification gives a
written reason. A required section MUST NOT be silently omitted.

These obligations are governed by `SPEC-001`, `SPEC-003` and `SPEC-004`.

## 1. Identity

Provide the title, stable specification identifier, version, `draft` status,
primary profile and accountable owners.

## 2. Purpose And Problem Statement

Describe the recurring civic or integration problem addressed. Explain why a
reusable contract is needed instead of one product-specific implementation.

## 3. Scope And Boundaries

List scope, responsibilities and non-responsibilities. State which decisions
belong to another Module, Blueprint, Design System pattern or Shared Service.

## 4. Assumptions And Dependencies

State assumptions, required dependencies and optional dependencies. Reference
contracts rather than named vendors.

## 5. Actors And Consumers

Identify civic actors, implementing teams and systems that depend on the
contract. Distinguish human actors from software consumers.

## 6. Domain Concepts And Terminology

Define the terms needed to read the specification consistently. Reuse Civic
Stack terminology and explain any new concept.

## 7. Interfaces And Integration Expectations

Describe the interfaces exposed or consumed, their ownership and the stability
expected by integrators.

## 8. Data Exchange Expectations

Describe exchanged information, identifiers, schema ownership, privacy
constraints and compatibility expectations.

## 9. Cross-Cutting Obligations

Identify applicable API, event, authorization, auditability, interoperability,
accessibility, localization, security and operability requirements.

## 10. Conformance And Evidence

List applicable normative requirements and the inspectable evidence expected
for each one. Explain why any cross-cutting requirement is not applicable.

## 11. Risks And Trade-Offs

Document relevant civic, technical, privacy and interoperability risks. State
the trade-offs accepted by the specification.

## 12. Open Questions

List unresolved decisions that do not prevent use of the current draft. Each
question must have an owner or a stated decision point.

## 13. Change History

Record version, date and a concise description of material changes. Preserve
references to deprecated requirements.

## Authoring Checklist

Before review, confirm that the specification:

* declares exactly one primary profile;
* contains every common section or a reason it is not applicable;
* uses normative keywords consistently;
* assigns unique, stable identifiers to normative requirements;
* identifies evidence for every applicable mandatory requirement;
* states responsibilities and non-responsibilities;
* separates required dependencies from optional dependencies;
* contains no product- or vendor-specific requirement unless the scope
  explicitly demands it;
* distinguishes normative requirements from examples;
* records risks, trade-offs and open questions.
