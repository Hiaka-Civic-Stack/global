Participation owns the lifecycle and public identity of a civic process. It
defines when participation is possible and publishes the process-level outcome
without owning contributions, individual responses or commitments.

## Responsibilities

* process identity, metadata and accountable institution;
* stages, participation periods and lifecycle transitions;
* territorial scope and eligibility-policy references;
* enabled capability declarations;
* public process status and `ProcessOutcome`;
* stable references consumed by other Modules.

## Non-Responsibilities

Participation does not own contribution content, moderation, individual
institutional responses, deliberation, formal decision strategies,
commitments, verified legal identity or Shared Digital Service implementations.

## Consumers And Dependencies

Products, Blueprints and other Modules consume Participation through published
APIs and events. Authentication is required for protected actions. Identity or
verification, territory, document and notification services are conditional
ports. No specific Blueprint or provider is required.

## Actors

Public visitors read published processes and outcomes. Participants inspect
participation conditions. Institution managers configure and transition
processes. Authorized operators may archive records, while schedulers may
execute explicitly authorized temporal transitions.

## Risks And Trade-Offs

Keeping eligibility as a policy reference protects identity boundaries but
requires explicit adapter behavior. Owning the process outcome keeps lifecycle
transitions coherent while leaving contribution-level responses independent.

## Assumptions And Open Questions

The specification assumes trusted organization, territory and verification
contracts can be supplied through ports. No unresolved question prevents the
v0 contract; deployment-specific retention and service targets remain declared
implementation parameters.
