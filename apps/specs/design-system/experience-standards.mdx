---
title: "Experience Standards"
description: "Experience standards for Hiaka-compatible civic products."
---

Experience standards define what a civic product should make understandable,
traceable and accessible.

They are not API contracts. They are standards for civic experience quality.

## Process Status

A product should make process status visible and understandable.

Users should be able to tell whether a process is draft, published, open for
participation, closed, under response, decided, committed or archived.

## Actor And Responsibility

A product should identify the public actor behind a civic action.

Where relevant, the interface should distinguish:

* institution;
* facilitator;
* moderator;
* participant;
* public observer;
* responsible implementation actor.

## Eligibility And Scope

Users should understand participation scope before taking action.

The product should explain:

* who can participate;
* whether verification is required;
* what territorial scope applies;
* which dates or stages limit participation;
* what happens to submitted contributions.

## Public Evidence

Important public claims should point to evidence where possible.

Examples:

* consultation documents;
* institutional responses;
* decision rationale;
* commitment milestones;
* progress evidence;
* publication history.

## Accountability Progress

When a product tracks institutional commitments, progress should be visible
without requiring insider knowledge.

Progress should name:

* commitment;
* responsible actor;
* current milestone;
* latest update;
* evidence;
* next expected update.

## Localization And Long Content

Components and flows should support Malagasy and French content without layout
failure.

Standards should account for:

* long words;
* longer translated labels;
* multi-line actions;
* mobile screens;
* low-bandwidth reading contexts.

## Accessibility

Every stable pattern should document accessibility expectations.

At minimum, specs should describe:

* keyboard behavior;
* focus order;
* screen-reader labeling;
* contrast expectations;
* behavior under zoom;
* error and status messaging.
