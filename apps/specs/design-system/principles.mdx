---
title: "Civic Principles"
description: "Principles that guide Hiaka civic product experience."
---

Civic Design System decisions should be traceable to civic principles.

These principles are not visual preferences. They are product-quality criteria
for civic technology.

## Trust

Interfaces should make institutional actors, process state, deadlines and
responsibilities clear.

Users should understand:

* who is asking;
* who can participate;
* who decides;
* what happens next;
* where public evidence can be inspected.

## Inclusion

Interfaces should support different literacy levels, devices, bandwidth
conditions, languages and participation contexts.

Inclusion affects content structure, interaction design, device assumptions,
language length and accessibility behavior.

## Transparency

Products should expose process rules, eligibility, status changes and public
outcomes in ways people can inspect without internal knowledge.

Transparency is not only publishing data. It is making the public process
legible.

## Auditability

Important civic actions should leave visible traces when relevant:

* actor;
* time;
* state;
* reason;
* evidence;
* relationship to the public process.

Auditability is an experience concern as well as a technical concern.

## Territory Awareness

Civic products should represent territorial scope without hard-coding one
country or administrative hierarchy into the design language.

Madagascar-specific language can exist in product configuration or examples,
but the design standard should remain reusable.

## Localization

Products should support Malagasy and French from the beginning. English may be
used for developer-facing documentation.

Localization includes:

* long labels;
* translated process terms;
* different reading density;
* local civic vocabulary;
* formatting for dates, places and territories.

## Accessibility

Products should be keyboard-accessible, screen-reader friendly and readable
under realistic contrast, zoom, device and connectivity constraints.

Accessibility is part of civic inclusion, not a finishing pass.
